## 🇿🇦Serenity🇿🇦

Serenity is a small cross-border team working to get VivaChek drug/screening test kits into the South African market. Dr. Kudzai Nyamuomba is sourcing the kits (via a supplier named Frank) and driving strategy from the sourcing side, while Nicole Chitareni, based in Louis Trichardt, is the on-the-ground point of contact handling South African compliance and logistics. The group was created 8/22/26 and by the next day Nicole was already fielding early product documents — an account confirmation letter, an AR report, a tax PIN, and shortly after an ISO 13485/MDSAP certificate for the kits.

The regulatory path took a turn early on: Dr. Kudzai initially explored whether a single SADC member state's certification could unlock a certificate of origin usable across the whole region, potentially shortcutting the process. That question got resolved 8/27 when Nicole reached South Africa's regulator directly — the SADC angle helps, but a SAHPRA (South African Health Products Regulatory Authority) certificate or license is still required to distribute the kits domestically, and that became the critical path for the rest of the thread. The same day, the team decided not to register a new pharmaceutical entity — Nicole's existing, tax-compliant, CSD-registered company will trade as "MedSerenity" instead, since a fresh registration would cost more than double.

Compliance costs and timeline came into focus quickly: two vendors sent quotes the morning of 8/27, and the one discussed in detail — Pharmers — priced the full engagement at R55,000, with the SAHPRA application portion alone at R15,600 and a stated 15-working-day turnaround. Nicole flagged that the brochure appeared to include some items (like the ISO/MDSAP certification work) the team already has in hand, and asked for a fuller breakdown — a request that then sat unanswered through a follow-up on 8/31. A physical shipment of 5 test kits to Nicole, originally planned for 8/26, was also pushed back a week after she was briefly hospitalized with flu on 8/25 (she'd recovered by 8/27).

Pharmers finally responded and held a meeting with the team on 9/8 at 14:00 CAT, after which the group made a clarifying decision: they only need the SAHPRA application itself, not the optional consultation or collection-fee services. Progress on the vendor side still felt slow enough that, on 9/9, Nicole brought in a new contact — Norman Nyamuwomba — and asked him to visit SAHPRA's Radiation Control office in person in Tygervalley, Cape Town, since phone and email to the relevant department weren't getting answers. Norman confirmed the same day he'd make the trip and report back on the process, documentation requirements, licensing procedure, and timelines.

**Status: In progress — SAHPRA application scoped and priced, in-person follow-up underway to unblock the vendor relationship; product shipment still pending.**
